Paperwork says one thing.

Compare factory declarations with independent evidence.

The problem

Start with reality.

A GST officer can’t tell which factories are hiding production or inflating tax credits, because every document she checks is declared by the factory itself, while the independent evidence (power use, goods movement, supplier records, bank flows) sits in separate places and is never compared. If this worked, she would start each week with a short list of units whose paperwork contradicts physical reality, each contradiction spelled out, and honest-but-unusual factories cleared.

How it works

Different sources.
One clearer picture.

Production

Material balance, power proxies and stock movement test the declared output.

Movement

Dispatch records are reconciled against invoices, including repeated e-way bills.

Supplier

Supplier returns and invoice chains expose unsupported purchases and loops.

Financial

Bank flows are compared with declared sales and purchases.

A month is prioritised only when at least two source families fire.

SQL rules make the decision. The note explains evidence. An officer makes the judgment.

What it doesn’t do yet.

False positives happen.

Honest factories can be prioritised. Alerts support human review; they are not findings of fraud.

Evasive cheats can escape.

A cheat visible in only one family is not prioritised. The example above shows that trade-off.

Synthetic only.

Verification status is generated. Sophisticated forgers and real taxpayer data have not been evaluated. A rule-of-three bound assumes independent factory outcomes; it does not establish real-world safety. A cluster-bootstrap interval remains unmeasured.

More evaluation and limitations

Paired decoy FPR before and after clearing, a regenerated low-prevalence dataset, and wrong-clear cluster-bootstrap intervals remain unmeasured. Ablation intervals, a sophisticated-forger test and reproduction on a second machine remain open. Power and bank data require lawful access.
