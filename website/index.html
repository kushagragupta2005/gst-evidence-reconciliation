The problem

A GST officer can’t tell which factories are hiding production or inflating tax credits, because every document she checks is declared by the factory itself, while the independent evidence (power use, goods movement, supplier records, bank flows) sits in separate places and is never compared. If this worked, she would start each week with a short list of units whose paperwork contradicts physical reality, each contradiction spelled out, and honest-but-unusual factories cleared.

The method

Four conservation checks.
One evidence trail.

01

Material → output

Purchased inputs and power establish an expected production range.

02

Dispatch → invoice

Goods movement must have a corresponding invoice and unique e-way record.

03

Stock roll-forward

Opening stock + purchases + output − dispatches must equal closing stock.

04

Supplier chain

Purchase claims are checked against supplier returns and looping links.

Why one signal is not enough

Every case shows its evidence.

Production, movement, supplier and financial sources are separated. Two distinct families must fire before a case appears in the priority queue.

Limits and roadmap

Synthetic data only

It demonstrates a reproducible method and error measurement, not taxpayer risk estimates.

Human review required

The system ranks evidence contradictions. It never decides fraud or legal liability.

Lawful data access

Start with tax-system data: invoices, e-way bills and supplier returns. Add power and bank data only under lawful, authorised access.
