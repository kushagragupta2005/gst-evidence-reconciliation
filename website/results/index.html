Results · synthetic data

Measured.
Including the misses.

What the held-out synthetic tests show, and what they cannot tell us.

Monthly outcomes.

How we measured.

The development seed chose the threshold and factory queue rule. The held-out seeds test those frozen choices. Monthly recall asks how many active cheat months were flagged. False-positive rate asks how many honest months were flagged.

The confidence intervals resample whole factories, preserving their months together. They describe sampling uncertainty within this synthetic design, not accuracy on real taxpayers.

Months and factories
answer different questions.

A factory enters the queue only after enough prioritised months. This is different from flagging an individual month.

What does zero
actually tell us?

Take one check away.

Leave-one-check-out recall with every other check still present. A small change can reflect overlapping evidence, not a useless check. Post-hoc point estimates only; ablation intervals remain unmeasured.

When cheating
is less common.

Precision changes with prevalence. This analytic reweighting holds sensitivity and false-positive rate fixed; it is not a new low-prevalence experiment.

The errors matter.

Paired before/after-clearing decoy FPR and stock-aware/evasive subgroup comparisons remain unmeasured.
